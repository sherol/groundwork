12Groundwork phasesat a glance

Body-centered skills to steady your nervous system, recover from stress and build resilience.

Groundwork follows the twelve-phase healing trauma program in Peter Levine's work on how animals, and people, recover from threat. Each phase builds one skill, with a guided audio track. You learn it, practice it for a few minutes, and carry it into your week. New here? Read the introduction first.

Preparation: safety and resources

Phases 1–3

The base everything else rests on.

1

Safety & containment

Wake up the edges of your body so it feels like a container strong enough to hold what you feel.

Try: tap gently from your hands up to your shoulders, saying "this is my arm."
2

Grounding & centering

Feel the earth supporting you and find your center of gravity in your lower belly.

Try: press your feet into the floor and breathe into your belly.
3

Building resources

Gather the people, places, strengths and body areas that help you feel steadier, and feel them in your body.

Try: picture someone kind and notice where you feel it.

Tracking: the language of sensation

Phases 4–6

Noticing, following and finding the body's rhythm.

4

Felt sense to sensation

Move from your overall felt sense to precise sensations: warm, tight, tingly, heavy.

Try: ask "how am I overall?", then "where do I feel that?"
5

Tracking activation

Follow sensations, images, impulses, emotions and thoughts as activation rises, peaks and settles.

Try: track a 2-out-of-10 stress until you notice a sigh or deeper breath.
6

Pendulation

Feel your body's natural swing between contraction and expansion, and learn that ease always returns.

Try: rest in a calm spot, touch a tense spot briefly, then return.

Discharge: completing survival responses

Phases 7–10

Letting thwarted defenses finish, and finding strength.

7

Healthy aggression

Reclaim the protective power of the fight response, so it becomes strength instead of rage.

Try: push slowly against a wall and feel your strength.
8

Healthy escape

Free the flight response locked in your legs, so anxiety can turn back into purposeful escape.

Try: imagine running along a safe path to somewhere safe.
9

Strength & resilience

Come out of collapse and defeat into an upright, resilient body.

Try: slump for a moment, then lengthen your spine slowly.
10

Uncoupling fear

Feel stillness and heaviness as plain sensation, without fear, so the freeze can resolve on its own.

Try: notice a mild heaviness, then wiggle a finger to show you can move.

Completion: returning to balance

Phases 11–12

Back into the world, and into everyday life.

11

Orientation & engagement

Move from your inner world out to your surroundings, and into connection with people who help you settle.

Try: look slowly around the room, then recall a kind face.
12

Settling & integration

Let your system settle into a new balance, and build a simple daily practice you can keep.

Try: pick one tool each for morning, stressful moments and evening.

How to use the phases

  • Start with the introduction, then go in order the first time through. Each phase builds on the ones before it.
  • Spend about a week per phase, or longer if you need it.
  • Keep coming back to grounding and resourcing. They're skills for life, not steps you finish.
  • Rate your activation before and after each exercise, and bring your one-page report to your therapist.

Why a ground squirrel?

California ground squirrels live the whole cycle every day. They stand up to scan for hawks (orienting), drive off snakes (healthy aggression), dash to the burrow (escape), shiver back to life after winter stillness (resilience), and sploot belly-down on cool earth to rest (grounding). Their nervous systems move between alert and at ease many times a day, and that flexibility is what Groundwork practices.

The goal

Together, the 12 phases help your nervous system move between alert and at ease more freely, recover faster after stress, and feel more at home in your body and in your relationships.
