1Key ideas: the paper cut

Your body knows how to heal, and it wants to. It's on your side. But a paper cut won't heal if you rub dirt in it every day and keep splitting it open, no matter how much ointment you put on top. Gut healing works the same way. Stay curious: what's getting in the way? What do I need?

  • Be the private investigator. Keep a log of what makes you feel 10% better, or 10% worse. Diets and pills won't work as well if the nerves to the gut are sending the wrong messages.
  • Progress over perfection. This is complex, and you and your body are both doing your best. You will figure it out, and you're not alone in this.
  • The conductor's mood decides how the symphony plays. A calm nervous system lets the gut do its job.
  • Why SIBO keeps coming back: small intestinal bacterial overgrowth tends to return unless the underlying causes are addressed, such as stress, low stomach acid or enzymes, thyroid problems, or biofilm.

From the round table

  • 2 belly breaths before each meal.
  • Slow waaay down: hydrate, chew, be present with the food.
  • Sit down to eat, with a moment of gratitude or prayer.
  • Fork down, phone down, sit down. Close your eyes for the first minute and really taste the food.
  • Self-compassion: you and your body are trying your best.

2Calm the gut–brain connection

There are more nerves going to the gut than in the entire spinal cord. The gut won't work properly until the signals from those nerves are calm, and say safety and peace.

  • Before every snack and meal: 2 slow belly (diaphragmatic) breaths. Your "software" needs to be online for the "hardware" to work. The 4-7-8 breath is great for digestion too.
  • Daily, for 12 weeks: gut-directed hypnotherapy with the Nerva app ↗. Research suggests it can work about as well for symptoms as a low-FODMAP diet, and doing both together may be even better. It has a free trial, and your clinician may be able to arrange a discount or financial help.

3Stomach acid support

Your baking soda test from Session 1 is a rough, ballpark test. It just helps you investigate whether acid support during meals could help. Always follow your intuition first, and talk with your clinician before starting.

Burped within 2 minutes, all three days?Your acid levels are probably fine for now.
Didn't?That's fine. Your clinician may suggest a trial of apple cider vinegar or betaine (below), and may order blood tests to look for causes of low acid.

Apple cider vinegar (most people can try this)

Use an organic apple cider vinegar "with the mother." Take 1 teaspoon diluted in ½ cup of water, up to 3 times a day, right before meals. You can work up to 1 tablespoon at a time if you like. Watch for changes over 1 week: less bloating? Better stools? Take notes. If you have methane-type overgrowth, it can make constipation worse.

Or: betaine with pepsin (with your clinician's guidance)

For people who didn't burp in the baking soda test, a betaine trial is a better way to check for, and treat, low stomach acid (which can cause bloating and IBS symptoms soon after eating). Start with 1 capsule at the start of each meal, and increase slowly, up to the most your clinician recommends, until:

  • you notice clear relief from gas and bloating: stay at that dose, or
  • you feel pain, burning or nausea: drop back to a dose that's comfortable. Don't push through discomfort.

This is a discovery process. The goal is a comfortable dose where you feel a clear benefit with no side effects. High-protein meals need more.

Discomfort with even a little vinegar or one betaine capsule? That may be a clue to an inflamed stomach lining or histamine sensitivity. Stop, and wait for Session 3 (healing the gut lining) before trying again.
Take a daily PPI (such as Prilosec or Protonix)? Don't stop it on your own. Talk with your clinician about tapering safely, and never stop it if you've been diagnosed with Barrett's esophagus.

4Digestive enzymes

Most people can try this. After you understand your acid needs, add 1–2 digestive enzymes at the start of each meal for 1 week (at the same time as betaine, if you take it). Does your digestion improve? If yes, continue. If not, stop.

Try at least 2 different brands to find one that suits you. Some that the group discussed:

NOW Super EnzymesNot vegetarian.

FODZYME or FODMATE: many people with bloating or IBS find these helpful, especially together with a low-FODMAP diet. Take one at the start of each meal and notice any improvement at all.

5Elimination diet trials

Get to know your gut and what it needs right now, not forever. Try one of these for about 6 weeks, and note what improves even a little. Progress over perfection. An AI assistant can help you make shopping lists and meal plans, and the FIG app ↗ can help you plan around your needs.

Gluten-freeEspecially for IBS, IBD, autoimmune, skin, allergy or headaches. Avoid wheat, barley and rye (most breads, cereals and pastas). Naturally gluten-free: rice, quinoa, oats, corn, beans, sweet potatoes, and all vegetables, fruit, nuts, seeds, olive oil and protein. Try lentil pasta, Mary's Gone crackers, Siete almond flour tortillas, or Simple Kneads or SRSLY bread.
Dairy-freeEspecially for joint pain, bloating or IBS. Go 2 weeks with no dairy and see how you feel. Then add back small amounts of A2 dairy (goat or sheep cheese or yogurt) and see if you tolerate it.
Low sugarGo 2 weeks with very little refined sugar. What shifts?
Reflux (GERD)-friendlySome foods loosen the valve that keeps acid out of the esophagus: coffee, fizzy drinks, chocolate, peppermint, garlic, onions, and fatty, spicy or fried foods. Notice whether citrus and tomatoes bother you too. Ask your clinician whether a small dose of melatonin at bedtime might help with overnight reflux.
Low FODMAPEspecially for bloating, IBS or SIBO. Cuts back on certain fermentable carbohydrates. See Monash University's FODMAP guide ↗; ModifyHealth ↗ delivers low-FODMAP meals.
Low histamineEspecially for IBS, pain, headaches or migraines, skin, allergy, or food reactions that seem inconsistent. More: fresh vegetables, fruit, whole grains, fresh meat, eggs, dairy. Less: aged, cultured and fermented foods, leftovers, processed foods and additives; walnuts, peanuts, tomatoes, spinach, eggplant, avocado, pineapple, strawberries and most citrus (a little lemon or lime is sometimes fine); alcohol, black tea, chocolate, cured meats and aged cheeses. A DAO enzyme with the first bite of each meal can help during the trial. See the SIGHI histamine elimination diet leaflet ↗. If cutting histamine helps a lot, ask your clinician about testing for SIBO.

Trouble swallowing? See a gastroenterologist (GI) for an upper endoscopy. If you're diagnosed with eosinophilic esophagitis, a 6-food elimination diet (gluten, dairy, egg, nuts, seafood and soy) is worth trying with their guidance.

Log what you're trying, with doses and brands, under Habits and trials in the Daily log.

6Tests your clinician may suggest

Depending on your symptoms, your clinician may order some of these. Follow the instructions you're given, and don't stop any medication without checking with them first.

  • Low stomach acid: fasting blood tests for vitamin B12 and intrinsic factor antibody, to rule out autoimmune gastritis.
  • H. pylori breath test (an infection that can affect stomach acid).
  • SIBO breath test for bloating, diarrhea, constipation or pain. It's done at home, needs a special diet the day before, and takes about 3 hours. The kit contains a glucose drink, so tell your clinician if you have diabetes.
  • Stool tests (calprotectin and elastase) for chronically loose stools, done with your next loose stool.
  • Celiac panel, if you haven't had one in the last 3 years. Keep eating gluten until the test, or it can give a false negative.
Getting ready for an H. pylori breath test

These medicines can hide H. pylori and cause a false-negative result. Ask your clinician before stopping any of them.

  • Antibiotics: stop at least 4 weeks before the test.
  • Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) such as omeprazole, lansoprazole or pantoprazole: stop at least 2 weeks before.
  • Bismuth (such as Pepto-Bismol): stop at least 2 weeks before.
  • Fasting: nothing to eat or drink except water for at least 6 hours before (usually the morning after an overnight fast).
  • On the day: no smoking, gum or mints.
  • The test drink contains aspartame (phenylalanine). Tell your clinician if you have PKU or diabetes.

Food sensitivity (IgG) testing usually isn't helpful. It isn't the same as allergy (IgE) testing. IgG mostly reflects what you've been eating, not an intolerance, so you'll tend to test "positive" to your usual foods.

1Key ideas

  • Investigate. The gut is complex, and each person's is different. Notice what makes a 10% better day.
  • Self-compassion. Your digestive system is doing the best it can with what it's been given.
  • It's connected. Gut flares can come with whole-body symptoms: anxiety, joint pain, muscle aches, fatigue, migraines.
  • Acceptance. Meeting the present moment as it is, without resistance, opens a path to healing.

2A calm conductor at every meal

There are more nerves in your gut than in your entire spinal cord. Digestion runs best in "rest and digest" mode, so your nervous system (the software) needs to be online for your gut (the hardware) to do its job. The symphony plays far better with a calm conductor.

  • Breathe first: take 2–4 slow belly breaths before every meal and snack. The 4-7-8 breath is great for digestion too.
  • Sit down, fork down, phone down. How relaxed can you get in one minute?
  • Chew each bite 30–50 times. Chewing until your food is smooth signals the rest of your gut to release its juices, and sets digestion up for success.
  • Bonus: try closing your eyes and chewing calmly. What do you notice?

3A tour of digestion

Watch: How your digestive system works (TED-Ed) ↗. To go deeper: a more detailed walkthrough ↗.

SalivaYou make 1–1.5 liters a day, so drink enough water.
MouthChewing well signals your stomach, pancreas and gallbladder to get ready.
EsophagusThe lower esophageal sphincter keeps stomach acid from coming up. It works best with an active vagus nerve (breathing, relaxing), a strong diaphragm and the right acid levels. Common reflux (GERD) triggers: caffeine, alcohol, high-fat meals, peppermint, chocolate, smoking and nicotine, and stress. With a hiatal hernia, raising the head of the bed can help.
StomachFor some people, too little stomach acid is behind reflux-like symptoms. Aging, stress, trauma and illness can all lower it. Low acid can lead to poor nutrient absorption and problems further down: bloating, recurring SIBO, diarrhea and an unbalanced microbiome.

4Fiber: food for your good bacteria

Fiber feeds the "good" bacteria in your gut, and much of health starts there. Large studies link more fiber with:

Longer lifeAbout 10% lower risk of death from any cause for each extra 10 g a day.
Heart and metabolismAround 30 g a day is linked with about 25% less cardiovascular death, steadier blood sugar after meals, less diabetes and fatty liver, and easier weight management.
Lower cancer riskUp to 40% less colon cancer at 30 g a day, and lower risk of several other cancers.
ImmunityMost of the immune system lives in the gut: fewer colds, and less autoimmune and Crohn's disease. Fiber also binds some pesticides from food so they're carried out.
Gut liningFiber raises short-chain fatty acids such as butyrate, which feed the gut lining. A healthy lining is like cheesecloth: good things through, bad things kept out. Inflammation widens the holes ("leaky gut"), which can show up as food sensitivities, joint pain and brain fog.
Bowel balanceLess diverticulitis, and steadier IBS, diarrhea and constipation.

Goal: 25 g a day. (Our ancestors ate 50–100 g.) Measure it for one week. You need about 7–8 servings of "healthy things" a day. Food is the best source when you can; if high-fiber foods don't agree with you yet, a fiber supplement can "train" your gut.

Tip: tell an AI chatbot everything you ate today, and ask how much fiber that was. Or use the fiber estimator in the Daily log.

Start very, very slowly

A sensitive gut needs time to get used to fiber. If you get bloated, cut back or split the dose, but don't stop completely.

OptionalBaking soda stomach acid test

Only do this if your clinician has asked you to. If you take a PPI (such as omeprazole/Prilosec), don't do it unless your doctor has said you can safely stop for 2 weeks. If you take Pepcid or Tums, try skipping the dose the night before.

  1. First thing in the morning, before eating or drinking, mix ¼ teaspoon of fresh baking soda into 4 oz of cold water.
  2. Drink it and start a timer. Note how long it takes to burp. Stop timing at 5 minutes.
  3. Repeat for 3 mornings in a row, or as many days as your clinician asks. Log each morning below.

In theory, burping within 1–2 minutes suggests enough stomach acid; burping after 3 minutes, or not at all, may suggest low acid, which your clinician may want to look into.

Choose a result, type how long it took, or both (for example 1.5 or 1:30). Choose No baking soda for a morning you drank the water without it, and still type the minutes if you burped.
