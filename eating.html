Tool 4 · Gut

Integrative Eating

Your gut is complex and amazing, and when it's out of balance the answer is usually more than a single pill. Calm meals, slow chewing, steady fiber and careful noticing all add up.

Be the private investigator: observe yourself and keep a log. What makes you feel 10% better, or 10% worse? Progress over perfection.

My body wants to heal. My body is on my side. My body is amazing!

1Key ideas

  • Investigate. The gut is complex, and each person's is different. Notice what makes a 10% better day.
  • Self-compassion. Your digestive system is doing the best it can with what it's been given.
  • It's connected. Gut flares can come with whole-body symptoms: anxiety, joint pain, muscle aches, fatigue, migraines.
  • Acceptance. Meeting the present moment as it is, without resistance, opens a path to healing.

2A calm conductor at every meal

There are more nerves in your gut than in your entire spinal cord. Digestion runs best in "rest and digest" mode, so your nervous system (the software) needs to be online for your gut (the hardware) to do its job. The symphony plays far better with a calm conductor.

  • Breathe first: take 2–4 slow belly breaths before every meal and snack. The 4-7-8 breath is great for digestion too.
  • Sit down, fork down, phone down. How relaxed can you get in one minute?
  • Chew each bite 30–50 times. Chewing until your food is smooth signals the rest of your gut to release its juices, and sets digestion up for success.
  • Bonus: try closing your eyes and chewing calmly. What do you notice?

3A tour of digestion

SalivaYou make 1–1.5 liters a day, so drink enough water.
MouthChewing well signals your stomach, pancreas and gallbladder to get ready.
EsophagusThe lower esophageal sphincter keeps stomach acid from coming up. It works best with an active vagus nerve (breathing, relaxing), a strong diaphragm and the right acid levels. Common reflux (GERD) triggers: caffeine, alcohol, high-fat meals, peppermint, chocolate, smoking and nicotine, and stress. With a hiatal hernia, raising the head of the bed can help.
StomachFor some people, too little stomach acid is behind reflux-like symptoms. Aging, stress, trauma and illness can all lower it. Low acid can lead to poor nutrient absorption and problems further down: bloating, recurring SIBO, diarrhea and an unbalanced microbiome.

4Fiber: food for your good bacteria

Fiber feeds the "good" bacteria in your gut, and much of health starts there. Large studies link more fiber with:

Longer lifeAbout 10% lower risk of death from any cause for each extra 10 g a day.
Heart and metabolismAround 30 g a day is linked with about 25% less cardiovascular death, steadier blood sugar after meals, less diabetes and fatty liver, and easier weight management.
Lower cancer riskUp to 40% less colon cancer at 30 g a day, and lower risk of several other cancers.
ImmunityMost of the immune system lives in the gut: fewer colds, and less autoimmune and Crohn's disease. Fiber also binds some pesticides from food so they're carried out.
Gut liningFiber raises short-chain fatty acids such as butyrate, which feed the gut lining. A healthy lining is like cheesecloth: good things through, bad things kept out. Inflammation widens the holes ("leaky gut"), which can show up as food sensitivities, joint pain and brain fog.
Bowel balanceLess diverticulitis, and steadier IBS, diarrhea and constipation.

Goal: 25 g a day. (Our ancestors ate 50–100 g.) Measure it for one week. You need about 7–8 servings of "healthy things" a day. Food is the best source when you can; if high-fiber foods don't agree with you yet, a fiber supplement can "train" your gut.

Tip: tell an AI chatbot everything you ate today, and ask how much fiber that was. Or use the fiber estimator on the Daily log tab.

Start very, very slowly

A sensitive gut needs time to get used to fiber. If you get bloated, cut back or split the dose, but don't stop completely.

    Practice1-minute pre-meal breath

    Before you eat: sit down, put your fork and phone down, and follow along. Three rounds of 4-7-8 breathing take about a minute.

    ·Ready when you are

    Breathe in through your nose for 4, hold for 7, breathe out slowly through your mouth for 8. If holding is uncomfortable, shorten the counts and keep the out-breath longest. Then take your first bite slowly, and chew it 30–50 times.

    OptionalBaking soda stomach acid test

    Only do this if your clinician has asked you to. If you take a PPI (such as omeprazole/Prilosec), don't do it unless your doctor has said you can safely stop for 2 weeks. If you take Pepcid or Tums, try skipping the dose the night before.

    1. First thing in the morning, before eating or drinking, mix ¼ teaspoon of fresh baking soda into 4 oz of cold water.
    2. Drink it and start a timer. Note how long it takes to burp. Stop timing at 5 minutes.
    3. Repeat for 3 mornings in a row, or as many days as your clinician asks. Log each morning below.

    In theory, burping within 1–2 minutes suggests enough stomach acid; burping after 3 minutes, or not at all, may suggest low acid, which your clinician may want to look into.
